# Privacy Policy — Kairos

_Last updated: October 31, 2026_

This policy describes how the Kairos app for iPhone and Android ("the app")
handles your information.

## Summary

Kairos has no user accounts and no server of its own, and it does not send your
personal information to the developer. Almost everything the app does happens
on your device. To show conditions, the app sends locations — where you are, and
any places you add — to the weather, tide, river, and map services listed below.
The developer does not collect, store, or sell your data, and the app has no ads
and no analytics.

## Location

The app uses your device's location to find the conditions where you are. To get
those conditions, your coordinates are sent to the services listed under
"Third-party services," which return forecasts, tides, and other data for that
place. On Android, the app asks for approximate location only.

To show the name of where you are (for example, "Arcata"), the app uses your
phone's built-in place-name lookup. On iPhone that is Apple's; on Android it is
your device's location provider, which is usually Google.

Your location is used only to fetch conditions and name the place. It is not
sent to the developer, is not linked to an identity, and is not used for
tracking or advertising. You can deny or revoke location access in your device
settings; the app's location features will be limited without it.

## Places you add

Spots you add — a surf break, a river gauge, a trailhead, a mountain — are stored
on your device. Their coordinates (and, for rivers, the gauge number) are sent to
the same services to fetch the conditions there.

On iPhone, maps are drawn with Apple Maps, and when you search for a place, your
search text is sent to Apple Maps. Topographic maps, on both platforms, come from
the U.S. Geological Survey: map tiles are requested for the area on screen, and
an offline map you choose to save is downloaded once and kept on your device.

## Information stored on your device

The following are stored only on your device (and in your device backup — iCloud
on iPhone, Google backup on Android — if backups are on). None of it is sent to
the developer:

- The activities, preferences, and spots you set up.
- Activities you commit to, and what you record about how they went.
- A history of recent scores, used to judge how unusual current conditions are.
- Which notifications you opened or dismissed, used on your device to adjust how
  often an activity is suggested. You can restore an activity in its settings.
- The most recent conditions and your last known location, so the app opens
  quickly and works without a signal.
- Offline maps you choose to save.

Removing the app removes this data.

## Calendar

With your permission, the app reads your calendar to find free and busy times so
it can suggest windows that fit your schedule. On iPhone it can also add an event
for an activity you commit to. On Android it only reads; adding an event opens
your calendar app, which asks you to confirm. Your calendar contents stay on your
device and are not sent to the developer or to any service.

## Motion and fitness (iPhone)

With your permission, the app reads recent motion and activity data to gauge how
active you've been, which it uses on your device to adjust when it suggests
things and notifies you. This data does not leave your device. Android does not
use it.

## Notifications

Notifications are created on your device. The app does not use a remote push
service, so no device token is shared with anyone. On Android, a periodic
background check fetches conditions for the last place you opened the app,
on your device.

## Sharing

When you share an activity, the link carries its settings in the part of the web
address after "#", which browsers do not send to any server. The link opens
Kairos, or, for someone who doesn't have it, a page hosted on GitHub Pages; like
any website host, GitHub may log basic request details such as an IP address.

When you invite someone to an activity, the message may include a Google Calendar
link and a map link with the time and place you chose. They go only to the people
you send the message to.

## Third-party services

When the app fetches conditions or maps, it sends the coordinates needed to these
services. Like any internet request, each request also carries your device's IP
address, which the service may log under its own privacy policy. Nothing that
identifies you, such as your name or contacts, is sent.

- **Apple Weather (WeatherKit)** — weather and weather alerts, on iPhone. See
  Apple's privacy policy and WeatherKit terms.
- **Apple Maps** — maps, place search, and place names, on iPhone.
- **Open-Meteo** — weather and sunrise and sunset on Android; air quality,
  marine and surf forecasts, snow, and elevation on both. Data licensed under
  Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0).
- **NOAA Tides & Currents (CO-OPS)** — tide predictions (U.S. government).
- **USGS Water Services** and the **NOAA National Water Prediction Service** —
  river flow and flow forecasts for river gauges you add (U.S. government).
- **USGS The National Map** — topographic map tiles and offline maps (U.S.
  government).
- **Your Android device's location provider** — the name of your location, on
  Android.

The app does not include advertising, analytics, or crash-reporting SDKs. If you
have chosen in your device settings to share analytics with app developers,
Apple or Google may give the developer anonymous, aggregated usage figures and
crash reports; you can turn that off in your device settings.

## Data we do not collect

The app does not collect personal information for the developer, does not use
tracking or advertising identifiers, does not show ads, and does not sell or
share your data for marketing.

## Children

The app is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information
from them.
